Tip: dimensions under each shape come from the scale above. Change “1 square = ft” and every label updates. Tap a shape with Select to move or delete it. Nudge a selected shape with the ◀▲▼▶ buttons or arrow keys (Shift + arrow = a full square). Grow or shrink it with ⤢/⤡ or the +/− keys — it resizes around its center. Tap ⊞ Snap (or press S) to pull a selected shape back onto the nearest grid square. Tap ◎ Center (or press C) to center a selected shape in the current view. Use ⤒ Front / ⤓ Back (or F / B) to layer a selected shape on top of or behind overlapping shapes. Tap ⌂ Origin (or press O) to move a selected shape so its top-left lands on the origin (0,0). Tap 📐 Exact Size (or press E) to type an exact size in feet for a selected shape — width × height for a rect, diameter for a circle, length for a line or wall — resized around its center. Tap ⇋ Flip H / ⇅ Flip V (or press H / V) to mirror a selected line or wall around its center — handy for flipping a slope or run direction; rects and circles are symmetric, so a flip leaves them unchanged. Tap 📍 Exact Pos (or press P) to move a selected shape to an exact position in feet — its top-left X and Y from the origin. Select a text label and tap ✏️ Edit Label (or press T) to rename it in place without deleting and retyping it. Use ↑ Forward / ↓ Backward (or ] / [) to move a selected shape just one layer — swapping with the shape right above or below it — instead of jumping all the way to the front or back. After panning around with ✋ Pan, tap 🧭 Reset View (or press 0) to jump the view straight back to the origin. Tap 🔍 Fit View (or press 9) to center the whole sketch in the view at once — handy after drawing or loading shapes that landed off-screen. Tap 🎯 Focus (or press L) to center the view on just the selected shape without moving it — handy for jumping straight to one shape in a big sketch. Tap ⭐ Keep Only (or press K) to keep just the selected shape and delete every other shape — handy for isolating one part of a sketch; Undo brings the rest back. The Area readout in the status bar shows the selected shape's area and perimeter in real feet — a rect gives its floor area and wall run, a circle its area and circumference, and a line or wall just its length — handy for quick material estimates. The Angle readout next to it shows how steep a selected line or wall runs, from 0° (level) to 90° (straight up) — handy for checking a ramp, pitch, or brace angle at a glance. The Total readout adds up the whole sketch — combined floor area of every rect and circle plus the combined length of every line and wall — handy for a quick whole-job material estimate without adding shapes up one by one. The Bounds readout next to it shows the whole sketch's overall footprint — its combined width × height in feet from the leftmost to the rightmost and topmost to bottommost edge — handy for checking whether a layout fits a room, sheet, or trailer at a glance. The Types readout next to it breaks the sketch down by shape — how many rects, circles, lines, walls, and labels it holds — handy for a quick count before ordering materials or checking nothing was missed. The Mid readout next to it shows the center point of the whole sketch in feet — the middle of its overall footprint — handy for finding the middle of a layout for a centered fixture, mount, or light. The Diag readout next to it shows the corner-to-corner diagonal of the whole sketch's footprint in feet — handy for checking a layout is square (equal diagonals) or whether it fits corner-to-corner in a trailer, sheet, or room. The Ratio readout next to it shows the aspect ratio of the whole sketch's footprint — its width divided by its height, like 1.33 : 1 for a 16 × 12 ft room — handy for scaling a layout up or down, matching it to a sheet or screen, or checking a photo or print will fit without stretching. The Perim readout next to it shows the distance around the whole sketch's footprint in feet — twice its width plus twice its height — handy for estimating trim, edging, fencing, or tape needed to go around a layout. The Cover readout next to it shows the footprint area of the whole sketch in square feet — its overall width times its overall height — handy for estimating flooring, sheet goods, paint coverage, or drop cloths needed to cover a layout's full footprint. The Fill readout next to it shows what share of that footprint the shapes themselves actually cover, as a percent — a single room filling its outline reads 100%, while gaps and open space inside the outline pull it down — handy for spotting wasted space in a layout or estimating how much of a sheet, floor, or lot is really in use. The Gap readout next to it shows that same open space as an absolute number — the footprint area minus the area the shapes themselves cover, in square feet — handy for estimating leftover sheet goods, offcuts, or how much open floor a layout leaves in real numbers instead of just a percent. The Edge readout next to it shows the total edge length of every shape in the sketch added together — each rect's perimeter, each circle's circumference, and each line or wall's length, in feet — handy for estimating trim, edging, tape, or cut length across a whole layout, not just around its outside footprint.
